The basics

What is composting?

Composting is a natural process where organic materials — food scraps, leaves, branches, sawdust — are broken down by bacteria and fungi into a dark, nutrient-rich fertilizer that feeds your soil and your plants.

  1. 1

    Collect materials

    Use scraps like veg, fruit, eggshells, coffee grounds, paper, leaves and grass. Skip meat, fats and dairy to avoid pests.

  2. 2

    Prepare the bin

    Place your materials in a well-ventilated compost bin or open pile with good airflow.

  3. 3

    Mix & moisten

    Turn and dampen the pile regularly so everything breaks down evenly.

  4. 4

    Let it heat up

    A healthy pile reaches 50–70°C, which speeds decomposition and kills weed seeds.

  5. 5

    Harvest compost

    In 3–6 months your scraps become crumbly, nutrient-rich compost ready for the garden.

The payoff

Benefits of composting

recycling

Less waste

Keeps organic waste out of landfills, cutting the methane and greenhouse gases it would otherwise produce.

potted_plant

Better soil

Enriches soil with organic matter, improving structure, water retention and the nutrients your plants crave.

savings

Save money

Free, homemade fertilizer means less spending on shop-bought soil conditioners and plant food.

diversity_2

More biodiversity

Feeds earthworms, microbes and beneficial organisms that keep your whole garden ecosystem thriving.
